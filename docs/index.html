Technical Architecture Deep Dive

Why pure native engineering matters for desktop automation.

🏎️ Headless Swift vs. Catalyst & WebKit

The official Home Assistant companion app is an iOS app ported via Mac Catalyst. Even when idling in the background, it runs an entire WebKit browser engine with multiple rendering processes, consuming 250MB–650MB of RAM and periodic GPU compositor wakeups. Mac2MQTT has zero webview dependencies. Written purely in native Swift with AppKit and Darwin, it runs as a lightweight ~30MB agent.

⚡ Push-Driven MQTT vs. HTTP Webhooks

The official companion app sends data over HTTP webhooks, requiring Home Assistant core API routing and cloud proxy hops. Mac2MQTT communicates directly with your local MQTT broker (e.g. Mosquitto) over persistent TCP/TLS. With Last Will and Testament (LWT), Home Assistant instantly knows the exact second your Mac sleeps or goes offline.

🔔 Kernel vnode Watchers vs. Polling

Instead of polling state on a 30-second timer, Mac2MQTT uses kernel-level file descriptor monitors (DispatchSourceFileSystemObject) on macOS's Assertions.json database. When you toggle Focus or Do Not Disturb, Home Assistant receives the updated state in under 50 milliseconds.

🖥️ Parity with Windows HASS.Agent 2

Mac2MQTT brings the beloved HASS.Agent experience to the Apple ecosystem. Both share identical Home Assistant MQTT Discovery standards (homeassistant/<domain>/<device_id>/<object_id>/config), meaning your Mac gets its own dedicated, first-class Device page in Home Assistant with zero manual YAML configuration.

Get Started in 60 Seconds

Simple, friction-free installation.

1

Install App

Download Mac2MQTT.dmg, open it, and drag Mac2MQTT into your Applications folder.

2

Configure Broker

Click the antenna icon in your menu bar, open Preferences, and enter your MQTT broker IP and credentials.

3

Automate!

Your Mac appears instantly in Home Assistant under Settings → Devices & Services → MQTT.
